Resolve a concrete evidence conflict, not an imagined one.
Apply when candidate passages disagree about the requested relation, date, edition, location or name.
1. Determine whether they concern different times, versions, roles or entities. Retain the clue's stated frame; an older officeholder can be correct for a dated clue even when a newer biography names someone else.
2. Prefer the passage that states the requested relation with the distinguishing constraints. Exact matching clue-and-answer records are useful evidence, but a mismatched date or negation disqualifies a superficial match.
3. Repeated snippets from the same source or copied wording do not constitute independent corroboration. Mention count and title prominence do not override a conflicting relation.
4. Compare the strongest alternative once. Change the answer only for an identifiable mismatch or stronger relevant evidence, not because another association is more familiar.
Fictional contrast: "Kelan led the institute in 1890; Sera succeeded in 1910" requires Kelan for 1890. Repetition of Sera in later snippets does not change that.
If support is incomplete, make the best supported inference without inventing a source quote. Do not require literal answer presence: legitimate inference and aliases may be absent from the context.
