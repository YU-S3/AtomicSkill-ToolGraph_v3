### 3. Handling Telegraphic Clues, Single-Locality Prompts, & Category Omissions
- **Single Locality / City Names**:
  - When a prompt consists solely of a city or locality name without a question sentence:
    - Major world cities (e.g., `Hamburg`) -> standard country: `Germany`.
    - U.S. towns/communities (e.g., `Timonium`) -> standard state: `Maryland`.
- **Centuries / Dates**:
  - If a clue is a telegraphic phrase or name-event pairing without an overt interrogative pronoun (e.g., `"Henry Ward Beecherfor adultery"`), it frequently stems from a time-period category (e.g., "CENTURIES", "NAME THE CENTURY", or "THE 1800s"). Check whether the event occurred in a prominent historical era or century (e.g., `the 19th century`).

### 4. Surnames, Mononyms, & Regnal Names vs. Full Names
- **Monarchs, Emperors, and World Leaders standardly known by Single Name**:
  - Write standard mononyms or regnal names without surnames:
    - Write `Napoleon` (NOT `Napoleon Bonaparte`).
    - Write `Charlemagne`.
    - Write `Richard III` (for regnal names/Shakespearean characters).
    - Write `Lenin` (NOT `Vladimir Lenin`).
- **Authors, Playwrights, Poets, Philosophers, and Scientists**:
  - Jeopardy! standard convention allows and records **SURNAME ONLY**:
    - Write `Genet` (NOT `Jean Genet`).
    - Write `Pascal` (NOT `Blaise Pascal`).
    - Write `Havel` (or `Vaclav Havel`).
- **Assassins, Notorious Criminals, and Infamous Figures**:
  - Presidential assassins and infamous historical figures are standardly known and recorded by their **FULL NAME** (First + Last, or full name):
    - Write `Charles Guiteau` (NOT just `Guiteau`).
    - Write `John Wilkes Booth`, `Lee Harvey Oswald`.
- **Modern Entertainers, Pop Culture Figures, & Art Patrons**:
  - Figures normally known by a full professional name retain both: `Billie Holiday`, `Peggy Guggenheim`, `Eddie Money`, `Richard Marx`.
  - Figures prior to a famous name change: `Cassius Clay`.
