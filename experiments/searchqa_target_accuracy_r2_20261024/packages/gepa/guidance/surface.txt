### 2. Number, Countability, and Pluralization Rules (Crucial)
Trivia benchmarks frequently expect standard plural forms for fruits, vegetables, snacks, and countable food items:
- **Fruits & Berries**:
  - When clues ask for fruits or berries (e.g., festival fruit, pie filling, dessert ingredients, crop types), **default to the plural form**:
    - "Coupes A L'Ananas are ice cream desserts made with this tropical fruit, not bananas" -> `Pineapples` (NOT `pineapple`; note the plural parallel with "bananas").
    - Oxnard festival for "this fruit" / Tart Toss / shortcake -> `strawberries` (NOT `strawberry`).
    - Blueberries, raspberries, blackberries, cranberries, cherries -> prefer plural unless the singular form is part of a strict proper title.
- **Snacks, Vegetables, and Common Food Items**:
  - Saratoga chips / thin fried snack -> `potato chips`.
  - French fries, peanuts, etc. -> standard plural form.
- **Countable Nouns vs. Single Specific Objects**:
  - Check the grammatical referent of the clue. When "they" or a plural pronoun refers to the food/object, or when contrasted with a plural noun, keep it plural (`potato chips`, `strawberries`, `pineapples`, `horses`).

### 5. Articles ("The", "A", "An") and Clue Phrasing
- **Grammatical Clue Referents ("this [noun]")**:
  - When the clue asks for an animal, coin, species, entity, event, or concept using "this [noun]" or "1 of these [nouns]", standard Jeopardy phrasing accepts either the bare noun or a/the:
    - "Knights of Malta paid ... annual rent of 1 of these birds" -> `falcon` (or `a falcon`).
    - "Of the U.S. coins currently being minted, it's the smallest in size" -> `the dime` (or `a dime` / `dime`).
    - "Steller's Sea Cow was a relative of this rare aquatic mammal found in Florida" -> `the manatee`.
    - "had the finish of this, won back then by... Spiridon Louis" -> `the marathon`.
    - "when it's this market that features a lengthy period of price drops" -> `the bear`.
- **Geographic Entities**:
  - Retain "the" for geographical features that conventionally take the definite article:
    - Rivers: `the Amazon`, `the Nile`, `the Mississippi`, `the Thames`.
    - Deserts: `the Sahara`, `the Mojave`, `the Gobi`.
    - Plural mountain ranges & archipelagos: `the Alps`, `the Rockies`, `the Adirondacks`, `the Bahamas`.
  - Do NOT append redundant generic geographic nouns (e.g., write `the Amazon`, NOT `Amazon River`).
  - States, provinces, and countries should be standard bare proper nouns: `Tennessee`, `Maryland`, `Germany`.
- **General Rule**: Strip leading articles ("the", "a", "an") for standalone titles and proper nouns unless grammatically intrinsic or required by the rules above.

### 6. Diacritics, Accents, and ASCII Normalization
- Strip all accent marks, carons, umlauts, acutes, graves, and tildes from proper nouns and foreign names:
  - Write `Vaclav Havel` (NOT `Václav Havel`).
  - Write `Dvorak` (NOT `Dvořák`).
  - Write `Moliere` (NOT `Molière`).
  - Write `Cezanne` (NOT `Cézanne`).
