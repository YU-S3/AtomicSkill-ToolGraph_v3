# LiveMath — HumanFrontier authored policy

This is an externally authored reference. Apply the relevant sections to the public task. The runnable Bank selects sections by public task form; this file exposes the complete readable policy.

## Identify the theorem-level decision

Read the hypotheses and what the question asks: a true statement, equivalence, classification, sharp bound, existence/uniqueness, or the strongest provable result. Compare the actual statements, not their letters, length or rhetorical confidence. Separate truth from strength: a stronger false statement is not an answer, while a true weakened statement may miss the requested theorem.

## Resolve the decisive difference between options

Keep common hypotheses fixed and isolate the clause where plausible options differ: implication versus equivalence, existence versus uniqueness, all versus some/sufficiently large, local versus global, uniform versus parameter-dependent, strict versus non-strict boundary, or an equality/classification clause. Check that clause using the relevant mathematical result or derivation. Do not reject a correct clause merely because you have not re-proved it, and do not add a converse just because the forward result holds.

## Use meta-options carefully, without letter bias

Identify a literal option saying that another option is correct but a stronger result can be proved; it may appear under ANY label. In this benchmark such an option is a serious empirical prior, not a throwaway alternative. Look for a real strengthening of a valid concrete option: sharp constants, endpoint/equality cases, converse, uniqueness, or complete classification. Prefer the meta-option when that strengthening is supported and not already expressed by a concrete option. Mere inability to prove maximality is not proof of a strengthening. Do not select the last letter by default or invent a meta-option when none is present.

## Check assumptions without over-cautious weakening

Match the theorem's actual assumptions, object and conclusion. Do not silently remove compactness/regularity/domain restrictions or upgrade a constant to uniform in time/parameters. Conversely, a cautious-sounding negation such as “need not be injective” or “no such model” is itself a claim needing support. Do not systematically prefer shorter, weaker or more cautious options; decide the mathematical difference, including any appended clause.

## Quantitative and asymptotic precision

For comparable bounds use the same variables, limit regime, hypotheses and implicit-constant dependencies. A smaller growth exponent improves an upper bound and a larger exponent improves a lower bound only in the appropriate asymptotic regime. Track log factors, derivatives, exceptional sets and endpoints. Do not equate an isolated exponent/epsilon symbol with an asymptotic comparison, and do not confuse pointwise, uniform, subsequential, limsup or liminf conclusions.

## Existence, uniqueness, equivalence and counterexamples

For ∀x∃y versus ∃y∀x track whether the witness depends on x; do not manufacture a finite truth table for an unknown predicate. For an equivalence check both directions under the stated domain. A necessary condition alone is not equivalent; weaker congruence moduli may admit extra cases. Existence does not imply uniqueness or realization of every possible parameter. A concrete admissible counterexample can refute a universal claim; failure to find one cannot prove it.

## Reach a decisive, well-formed answer

Spend effort on the discriminating clause and the relevant theorem, not an exhaustive re-derivation of every option. Use an exact calculation/counterexample helper only for an actual computational subproblem; otherwise reason directly. Re-read the selected option once for an unearned extra conclusion or missing sharpness. Choose the best-supported option even if uncertainty remains. Return exactly one supplied option label, with no explanation, extra alternatives or empty answer.
