# OfficeQA HumanStrong V2

## OA-Target
Interpret the exact requested quantity before choosing a formula. Identify the entity, measure, row population, observation dates, reporting vintage, nominal/real basis, units, direction, statistic, and output precision. Distinguish a balance (stock at a date) from transactions over a period (flow), total from component, gross from net, and issuance from amount outstanding. A similar-looking number is not a substitute. The supplied source pages are genuine public inputs, not guessed search summaries. If they already establish the answer, solve and submit without an obligatory search. Do not enumerate a large formal checklist in the answer.

## OA-Locate
Read the supplied page titles, table captions, headers and relevant rows first. Candidate filenames and source-page URLs narrow where to look; a bulletin's issue date is not the observation date of every row. If one fact is missing, identify that fact and search a distinctive table/measure/entity phrase in the candidate files. Use the actual advertised native schemas: grep uses regex pattern and a paths list; read uses a Unicode character offset. These are not the external baseline's line-based read or singular-path grep. Use returned offsets, then retain the table header, unit, row and relevant footnote together. Widen to another issue only when the candidate evidence lacks the required period or vintage. A zero-match search is not proof that a numeric value is zero. Stop search when sufficient evidence exists; do not repeat queries merely because no Program has yet run. scoped_search batches narrow retrieval when several windows are actually needed; its matches are retrieval evidence, not verified answers.

## OA-Table
Match the full hierarchy of a column header, not just a repeated word such as Total. Pair the requested row and period with the correct column; tables often contain parallel fiscal, calendar and monthly blocks, or separate public/official, marketable/nonmarketable and gross/net columns. A continuation page may depend on the previous page's header. Retain the column positions and heading before extracting values. Use table_extract only for a literal, unambiguous row/column mapping; pass the actual supplied or read text through its text input. If it reports ambiguity, inspect the header or use temporary Python with an explicit column mapping; do not force its first hit to be the answer. Preserve signs, parentheses, decimals, footnote markers and units. Blank, dash, unavailable and zero are distinct unless the source says otherwise. OCR can join adjacent cells or drop punctuation: compare local row/column context, never silently repair a value toward an expected answer. Transcription is not verified merely because a computation accepts the number.

## OA-Units
Carry the source unit with each operand. Convert both operands to a common unit before subtraction or ratio; cancel equal units without unnecessary scaling. Millions of dollars to dollars is multiplication by 1,000,000; thousands to millions is division by 1,000. Percent, decimal fraction, percentage-point difference and basis points are different quantities. A difference of two shares in percentage points is 100*(part2/total2-part1/total1), not a percent change in the part amounts. Convert 32nds quotations as whole + fraction/32; verify any sub-32nd convention. For currency conversion state the rate's direction and multiply or divide accordingly. For precious metals use the source's stated pricing basis and mass unit; troy pounds have 12 troy ounces, not 16. Never assume a historical conversion price from memory. treasury_transform performs supported conversions after these semantics are established; for an unsupported combination use one small temporary calculation.

## OA-Stats
Select the formula from the wording before calling a calculator. Signed difference = new-old; absolute difference = abs(new-old). Relative change from an explicit base = (new-base)/base. A symmetric relative/percentage difference uses abs(new-old)/((abs(old)+abs(new))/2); it is not base-relative growth. State which denominator the question defines; if explicitly defined, its definition takes priority. ratio = numerator/denominator. Multiply a fraction by 100 only for percent/percentage points, not a requested ratio. Annual log growth = ln(new/base)/elapsed_intervals; CAGR = exp(ln(new/base)/elapsed_intervals)-1. Observation count is not elapsed interval count. Arithmetic mean, geometric mean of growth factors, weighted average and a ratio of sums are not interchangeable. Sum exactly the intended rows and avoid counting a printed total plus its components. Population variance divides by n, sample variance by n-1; choose the requested convention. Keep full precision until final rounding. series_metrics exposes named operands for ratio/change/growth, values arrays for aggregate statistics, and explicit symmetric_difference/share_difference operations. Check its formula and unit output once; do not re-search correct operands just to duplicate a computation. If the needed formula is unsupported, calculate it transparently in temporary Python rather than misusing a nearly named operation.

## OA-Commit
When the question is answered by supported operands and the intended calculation, use the native runtime_step finish action immediately. Return the requested scalar, ordered list or date, with required precision and sign. Do not submit intermediate tables when asked for one statistic. Preserve numeric scale independently of notation: a percent result of 12.5 is not 0.125, whether or not a suffix is required. Omit explanatory text and unit/currency suffixes unless the requested answer template explicitly requires them. A requested list retains the question's order, not the chronological or numeric sort order. Use only the advertised native action fields; do not add an evidence_state or answer_candidate object. Provenance is retained by source/result refs without an extra bookkeeping call. Do not recalculate or replace a supported answer solely because the runtime enters finish-only mode.

## OA-Fallback
Use the smallest execution path that resolves the task. All five Programs are callable from the start; phase telemetry is advisory. Programs are optional and do not certify the semantics of supplied operands. For one simple supplied table, direct extraction plus series_metrics may suffice; for repeated reads use scoped_search; for many periods use period_ledger. Pass real source_refs where available. Public source-page refs in the request can be read with read_result and passed as arguments without a corpus search. Temporary execute_python is top-level code: ctx already exists; do not assign to ctx or define run(ctx, inputs), and do not assume an inputs variable. Persistent Programs instead use run(ctx, inputs) and their advertised schemas. Temporary Python can print JSON containing operand_values and answer. Internal ctx.call operations share the same task tool allowance. Do not use unsupported bash/network/direct corpus access. On a tool/schema rejection correct the specific argument once from its advertised contract; repeated unchanged retries do not add evidence. If necessary evidence is truly missing, preserve what is known and acknowledge the gap rather than fabricate a denominator or choose a proxy value.

## OA-Periods
List the required observations and their exact time basis before computing a series statistic. Distinguish endpoints, interval count, inclusive year count and all observations in a date range. For an annual growth calculation between years a and b, elapsed intervals = b-a. Match dates literally when weekly observations or month-end dates are requested; do not replace a specific date with the nearest row unless the question permits it. For relative dates such as second-last reporting week, enumerate actual reporting dates and select by that order. Keep one value and source for each required period; later bulletins may contain the full earlier series. Read only missing periods. period_ledger checks coverage and conflicting entries, but does not extract a table or decide fiscal/calendar semantics. Do not compute a full-range statistic from a silently shortened series, fill missing years by interpolation, or count multiple vintages of the same observation twice.

## OA-Fiscal
Use the table's period labels to distinguish fiscal years, calendar years, monthly totals and year-to-date amounts. Fiscal-year start/end conventions can change historically; use the applicable source definition, including transition quarters. A month in a bulletin date does not by itself identify the fiscal period. Do not sum cumulative year-to-date observations as independent monthly flows; difference adjacent cumulative totals only when their basis and vintage agree and that is the intended reconstruction. Annualize only if explicitly requested. Preserve a period label such as FY or CY on each operand during calculation so the two bases cannot silently mix.

## OA-Treasury
Read Treasury categories literally: issued, retired, redeemed, tenders received, tenders accepted, public debt outstanding, net cash raised, refunding, public holdings and government accounts describe different quantities. Use accepted bids for allocations, not tenders received; use cash/new financing rather than gross issuance when that is requested. Do not combine maturity buckets with totals that already include them. For interest rates distinguish coupon, yield, discount rate and effective rate and retain their day-count/price basis. Government-held amounts are not the same as Federal Reserve holdings unless the table defines them so. Gold assets/liabilities and monetary stocks can use different revisions and valuation bases; select the named table and stated unit, not a familiar historical price. Preserve footnotes excluding special issues, matured debt or particular accounts before comparing totals.

## OA-Advanced
For correlation and OLS, align x and y by identical observation periods and specify dependent versus independent variable; slope changes if they are reversed. Use all required observations and consistent units. For standardization distinguish population/sample sigma. A percentile requires a specified interpolation convention; do not claim a unique convention if the task leaves it ambiguous. Gini variants differ in finite-sample correction: use the requested convention and nonnegative complete observations. Box-Cox is (x^lambda-1)/lambda with log(x) at lambda=0; domain and inverse transform matter. For CPI-adjusted values use nominal * reference_CPI/current_CPI with matching period and stated index basis. Forecasts must respect the stated model order, trend/intercept, horizon and fitting convention; a generic linear trend is not a substitute for ARIMA. Check installed capabilities and use an explicit supported calculation; never invent coefficients or rely on a memorized forecast. A supported deterministic special case may be computed directly with its assumptions stated internally.

## OA-Revision
Treat publication vintage as part of a value's identity when the question names a report, issue, as-of date, preliminary or revised basis. Prefer the provided page matching that vintage. If two sources disagree, first compare observation period, scale, component coverage and revision label; do not average them or choose the value that looks plausible. Later revised figures are not automatically the right answer to a historical as-reported question. If no vintage is specified and an actual revision is relevant, retain the source's explicit revision context and use a coherent series. A file's publication year must not replace the year printed on a data row.
